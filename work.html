Talk · 2026
Online

Feedforward Neural Networks for Disability Income Pricing

Mid Atlantic Actuarial Meeting, November 2026. Forthcoming.

This session compares generalized linear models (GLMs) and feed-forward neural networks (FFNNs) from the perspective of practicing actuaries who must deliver models that are accurate, stable, explainable, and auditable. Using a pricing framework, we examine what each approach does well, where each can fail, and how to evaluate them in ways that align with actuarial governance (calibration, stability, tail behavior, and portfolio O/E). We will use a practical case study with high-cardinality categorical variables (e.g., region/occupation/employer) and climate-hazard covariates (heat and smoke proxies) to illustrate common real-world challenges: rare-event incidence, heavy-tailed severities, and drift over time. A key theme is that “better AUC” is not enough—pricing depends on probability calibration, segment adequacy, and stable performance across rolling time windows. We will also discuss hybrid deployment patterns (e.g., GLM frequency combined with FFNN severity) that often deliver the best balance of lift and governability. The goal is how to test, decide, and implement models that “actually work” under operational and regulatory constraints.

Podcast · 2026

Quantifying Mortality and Morbidity Impacts from Climate Risks: A Practical Framework for Decision-Making

With Dale Hall and Jérôme Crugnola-Humbert. Research Insights Podcast, Society of Actuaries. 01 September 2026.

How can actuaries assess mortality and morbidity risk when historical experience may no longer fully reflect a changing climate environment?

In this episode of the Research Insights Podcast, Dale Hall, Managing Director of Research at the Society of Actuaries Research Institute, welcomes Carlos Arocha, FSA, SCR, and Jérôme Crugnola-Humbert, SAV, IA, authors of the report Quantifying Mortality and Morbidity Impacts from Climate Risks: A Practical Framework for Decision-Making.

Carlos and Jérôme discuss how climate hazards, population exposure and vulnerability can translate into life and health outcomes and ultimately affect actuarial decision-making. They explore why historical mortality and morbidity data alone may be insufficient in a changing risk environment, how climate-related variables can be incorporated into mortality modeling, and what a case study examining France, Italy and Spain can teach actuaries about interpreting forward-looking scenarios.
The conversation also examines the “streetlight effect” and the “quantitative fallacy,” along with practical considerations for life and health insurers seeking to better understand and prepare for climate-related risks.

Listen to the episode and explore the full report, accompanying actuarial toolkit, and additional research from the Society of Actuaries Research Institute by visiting SOA.org and navigating to the Catastrophe & Climate research area.

Read the Report: https://www.soa.org/resources/research-reports/2026/climate-mortality-morbidity-framework/

Talk · 2025
Seattle, WA

Data-Driven Leadership: Navigating Climate Risk in Actuarial Work

With Brian Hinkle and Daniel Pribe. Society of Actuaries ImpACT Conference. 03 November 2025.

This session explores how actuaries can incorporate climate risk into practical decision-making when data are incomplete, uncertain, and highly scenario-dependent. It combines qualitative risk assessment with quantitative approaches, illustrating how climate variables can be integrated into actuarial models for mortality, morbidity, pricing, reserving, ORSA, and enterprise risk management. The presentation also examines a range of modelling techniques—including GAMs, Bayesian networks, survival models, and gradient boosting—and emphasizes the importance of interpretability, calibration, scenario analysis, and governance when translating climate signals into actionable actuarial insights.

Talk · 2025
Online

AI Under Regulation: The EU Sets the Standard

Society of Actuaries Emerging Topics AI Subgroup Call. 04 September 2025.

This session explores two practical dimensions of AI adoption for actuaries: understanding the emerging regulatory framework and using large language models effectively in model-building workflows. It summarizes key prohibited practices under the EU AI Act and then demonstrates how LLMs can support the development of auditable Excel models through clear problem definition, structured prompting, iterative refinement, and user validation. A case study on economic scenario generation illustrates how actuaries can use LLMs as collaborative tools to improve efficiency, documentation, and understanding while retaining control over model logic and outputs.
